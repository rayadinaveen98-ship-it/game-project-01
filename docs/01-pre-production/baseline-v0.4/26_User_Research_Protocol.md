# GP-01 Concept Lab field guide (real people required)
**Do not invent or backfill participant data.** Consent, anonymity and minimal data collection come first. No photographs, names, contact data or exact ages required. Anonymous codes are enough. Stop if someone wants to stop.

## Eligibility and bias
- Target player: someone who can reasonably play an Android game; include non-filmmakers in M tests.
- Recruit 8-12 first-time viewers per hypothesis for a fast comprehension screen, and 5-8 fresh people per hypothesis for paper-play (can overlap with comprehension only if disclosed).
- Do not tell players the desired mechanic or category ahead of the 5-second exposure.
- Prefer independent cohorts, or counterbalance concept order (`P D M`, `D M P`, `M P D`) across testers.
- Do not use the owner, researcher or assistant as substitutes for external players.

## Exact 5-second script
"I'm showing you a short game pitch for five seconds. I'll hide it and ask what you think players do. There are no wrong answers."
[Show only the corresponding single page from `32_Five_Second_Stimuli_v0.4.pdf` for 5 seconds; hide.]
"What is the player's goal?" [verbatim]
"What action would you try first?" [verbatim]
"How sure are you, from 1 to 5?"

Mark `clear`, `partial` or `not-understood` with the classification rule in the experiment brief; preserve the actual text. Never fix a failed first impression by adding an explanation and then marking it as successful.

## Exact 6-8-minute paper-play script
"Here's a short rule sheet and the pieces. Try to reach the goal, using whatever you think the rules allow. If you're confused, please say what you're thinking. I won't guide you unless the session is stuck."
Record: time to legal first action; legal moves; hints given; solved/unresolved; volunteered next attempt; 1-5 interest; one-sentence difficulty; strongest complaint; what action they believed changed the game state.

For M, reserve an additional blind viewer; present no private editing goal and ask interpretation openly before emotion labels. This avoids confirming the maker's intent through demand characteristics.

## Stop / rewrite signals
- Same fundamental rule misunderstood by >1/3 of first-time viewers; update the explanatory graphic, not just add words.
- Repeated illegal actions despite reading the short rules.
- P: physically counting valves is an arithmetic chore; D: correct answers reached by guessing; M: blind feedback is random/indifferent to order.
- Any test requiring the facilitator to explain a hidden assumption is a failed prototype specification, not a participant failure.

## Analysis rule
Keep raw responses and denominator, distinguish qualitative notes from measured outcomes; don't rank or select on a pooled score until comparable samples and content-pilot costs are available. Comprehension success and interest can conflict; report both.
