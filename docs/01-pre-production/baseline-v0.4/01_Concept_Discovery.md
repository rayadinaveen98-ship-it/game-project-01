# Concept discovery and selection method
**State:** Market and genre research v0.2 prepared, 8 candidate hypotheses drafted. Formal scorecard blank until adequate direct competitor/player evidence. See documents 13–15. No winner.

## 1. Explore, then select
Gather several concepts drawn from different fun patterns (e.g. short-session skill, puzzle with novel rule, simulation/tycoon, story mystery). Do not use popular genres as a proxy for a market opportunity. Each concept gets the exact same one-page card and evidence standard.

## 3. Comparison research
For each concept, document a minimum of 5 concrete comparators across successful games, poorly reviewed games, and new/emerging games. For each comparator capture store/platform link, date verified, screenshots for internal inspiration only, mechanics, pricing, review patterns, complaints, art approach and retention/discovery hypotheses. Clearly distinguish **observed evidence** from **our interpretation**. Do not import third-party copyrighted assets into the product.

## 4. Scoring rubric — equal quality/business priority
Enter 1 (weak) through 5 (strong) in `10_Concept_Scorecard.csv`; leave cells empty until backed by actual research. Weighted score = sum(score × weight) / 5, resulting in 0–100. Categories: fun potential (25), differentiation (15), feasible production (20), art feasibility (10), discovery/marketing (10), revenue compatibility (10), risk/cost safety (10). Weights are intentionally balanced: creative (fun 25 + differentiation 15 + art 10 = 50), business/execution (production 20 + discovery 10 + monetization 10 + risk 10 = 50). Low scores need remediation plans, not wishful thinking. Use this score as one input, not an automatic decision.

## 5. Prototype acceptance experiment
- Determine the single mechanic that needs proof. Remove UI and features unrelated to that mechanic.
- Define observer task, count of first-time testers, session length, questions and failure threshold **before** testing.
- Observe without teaching the mechanic unless onboarding is part of the experiment.
- Record what players actually do (confusion, repeat attempts, abandonment, delight), not only what they say.
- Decide **GO / ITERATE / KILL** against predeclared criteria.

## 6. Final concept approval
Before development, lock: approved pitch, chosen audience, primary platform, landscape/portrait, play session, core mechanic, explicit release scope, content estimate, and what will *not* be built. Register a signed-off decision in `07_Decisions.csv`.
