## Region and business implications
**India-first vs global-first is OPEN.** Indian Android users provide an accessible test population and potential early reach, but India-only IAP economics may be weaker than markets like the US. A global-ready game with readable iconography, minimal text requirements, graceful offline operation, and optional regional-language content could serve both; this is a hypothesis, not approved strategy. Translation/localization work, text rendering and cultural accuracy should be budgeted if narrative-heavy. 

### Genre comparison for a small initial studio
All judgments below are **analyst hypotheses**, not measured probabilities. Revenue prospect means whether the genre has proven spending patterns **somewhere**, not whether *our* game will earn money.

| Genre | Demand evidence | Potential business model | First-game content / technical burden | Discoverability hypothesis | Initial disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minimalist logic / spatial | High puzzle IAP, recent Indian arrow trend | Rewarded hints, chapter unlock, one-time premium; avoid forced ads | Low–Medium if mechanic original and solvability provable | 5–10-sec satisfying demos, daily challenges | **Deep exploration** |
| Evidence-based mystery | Named indie detective/escape games exist; no verified category market size | Premium chapters / optional hint ads | Medium, especially writing/localization and fair clue design | Mystery hooks and spoiler-safe reveals | **Deep exploration** |
| Tycoon / management | Examples of long-lived management sims; India simulation download interest | Premium, upgrade packs or gentle rewarded bonuses | Medium–High: balancing, UI, events, progression | Time-lapse transformations, decisions, shareable outcomes | **Deep exploration** |
| Hybrid casual arcade | Broad downloads but high competition and paid acquisition dependence | Ad-supported with optional cosmetics and unlocks | Low–Medium mechanics; high competition for attention | Short loops easy to demonstrate, difficult to sustain | Explore |
| Cozy restoration / sorting | Casual/merge spend growth, strong established incumbents | Decorative packs / chapter unlock / optional ads | High asset and content demand | Before-after transformations | Selective exploration |
| Atmospheric 2D adventure | Successful artistically distinctive indie precedents | Paid premium / episode packs | High handcrafted scene and animation load | Cinematic trailer / creator storytelling | Creative benchmark |
| 4X strategy / live-service RPG | Strong industry revenue | IAP plus ongoing live operations | Extremely high backend, economy, balancing, ongoing ops | Competitive audience costly to acquire | Exclude from *first* release scope |
| Shooter / 3D multiplayer | Established large markets | Service model | Extremely high networking, 3D art, anti-cheat and ops | Dominated by major studios | Exclude from *first* release scope |

## Production discipline: commercial model options
- **Premium paid:** strong with a self-contained experience, but raises first-download friction and demands convincing reviews/store presentation. Mini Metro and Game Dev Tycoon are evidence of a model, not expected revenue.
- **Free with voluntary rewarded ads:** most plausible for replayable puzzles, arcade, daily challenges; revenue depends on geographic ad rates, fill and actual players. Never rely on ad eCPMs guessed in a spreadsheet.
- **Free demo + paid chapters:** plausible for mystery/adventure but requires excellent early content and clear user value; use real Play billing only after game concept approved.
- **Hybrid ads + IAP:** plausible for casual simulations, but introduces economy/policy/QA costs. Do not launch a bloated shop before gameplay works.
- **No real-money wagering, deceptive bots, lootbox pressure or manipulative dark patterns** in the initial project.

## Visual-system feasibility
1. **Vector/minimalist 2D:** greatest consistency with procedural geometry and repeatable UI; challenge is feeling original rather than generic.
2. **Illustrated flat 2D:** strong for narrative, easy to create reusable backgrounds/portraits; challenge is character/scene consistency and localization text density.
3. **Pixel art:** coherent if tile size, palettes and sprite animation specs locked; content still expensive to animate across many situations.
4. **Isometric 2.5D:** attractive for miniature management, but every furniture/building/character addition multiplies asset demands and animation workload.
5. **Cinematic silhouette / illustrated adventure:** striking but lower asset reuse and high scene/animation cost; needs tighter episode scope.
6. **Low-poly 3D:** not forbidden, but lighting, modeling/rigging, effects and performance pipeline must be proved first.

## Red flags and what must be measured
- Market demand is not a conclusion about *product-market fit* for any proposed concept.
- A large category may have higher user acquisition costs, more content demands and better-funded competitors.
- Some named market estimators report gross IAP, others net IAP; don't combine them or infer ad income.
- Prefer **anonymous, consent-based, first-time player testing**. Separate qualitative onboarding reactions, actual play sessions and real retention; don't invent DAU or financial forecasts.
- For a first prototype, we can benchmark: 5-second clarity, ability to learn controls without explanation, desire to replay, level solution fairness, and asset pipeline reproducibility. Define sample counts and success criteria before a test.

## Outstanding research BEFORE final concept
1. Complete ≥5 direct competitors **per shortlisted concept**, noting current platform availability, entry price, review complaints, screenshots, accessibility, advertising and progression. Current named examples are orientation only, not that full audit.
2. User segment and region interviews. What game do they currently play, how long, and why did they stop? Segment genre-specific audiences; don't treat friends as representative market validation.
3. Thumbnail/5-second video message testing and first-session usability tests for top hypotheses.
4. Test actual art assets in a real game scene before approving the art bible.
5. Test whether APK/AAB can be built with user-free local installation.
6. Confirm budget ceiling, payment tolerance, regional language plan and Play developer account state when choosing distribution.
