# M | Montage interpretation - artistic mechanics without fake ground truth
**Status:** Research experiment, not an editing-game specification or finished illustration direction.

## Core action
One person receives three original, simple storyboard cards and a private desired emotional tone. They rearrange the cards into a three-shot scene. A **different person**, who never sees the goal, describes the scene and chooses an emotional interpretation from neutral categories. The designer observes whether meaning changes with shot order. No answer is asserted from the art alone.

| Trial | Original shot cards | Private maker aim | Blind viewer question |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 / Envelope | Neutral face; sealed envelope; empty chair | Unease | "What might this person be feeling, and why?" |
| M2 / Deadline | Running figure; clock near six; closing gate | Urgency | "What seems likely to happen next?" |
| M3 / Departure | Suitcase; small growing plant; departing train | Hope | "Does this sequence feel hopeful, sad, tense or unclear? Why?" |

**Procedure**
1. Give maker cards face-up in randomized order and one private aim. Their task is to arrange them. Note order, verbal intention and how long it took.
2. Remove the aim text. Show only the actual arranged sequence to a blind viewer. No narrator, film knowledge question, sound or timestamps.
3. Ask an open-ended story/emotion question before offering any labels. Capture answer verbatim. Then ask confidence 1-5. For comparison repeat later with a different order and new blind viewer.
4. Do not award a correct/incorrect grade based on agreement with a prewritten answer key. Small samples cannot establish that a sequence creates a specific emotion.

## Existing work / nuance
Montage and the Kuleshov effect motivate the experiment, but interpretation is affected by context and stimulus design. The 2024 behavioral/fMRI study reported valence differences under controlled sequences. Existing shot-order puzzle Actorle: Storyboard already asks players to reorder film frames, but that is chronological trivia, not blind emotional communication. Novelty remains unverified.

## Art unit estimate (not effort estimate)
Three sets x three illustrations = **9 schematic shot cards** in this paper test. For a later hypothetical eight-set V1, at least **24 finished cards** plus UI/outcome variations would be needed, before alternate palettes, animation and localization. Count is a content-unit estimate only; time, price and fun remain unmeasured.
