# GP-01 — Concept slate: 8 hypotheses, **not** a chosen game
**Version 0.2 · 2026-11-07**

All eight are deliberately tentative *working descriptions*. Working names are unverified and may conflict with existing trademarks or game names. No claims of novelty or commercial success have been established. Target audience, platform and duration are assumed for comparison only. Concept cards are the first-pass discovery stage, not final GDDs. Business models are hypotheses.

## C01 — Lumen Grove
**Genre:** spatial / environmental puzzle  |  **Art:** premium vector geometry + small living dioramas  |  **Proposed sessions:** 3–7 minutes.
- **Promise:** Grow an impossible miniature ecosystem by carefully redirecting heat, water and light using a few movable shapes.
- **One action:** Rotate/position one interlocking reflector/path tile.
- **Core loop:** See organism need → arrange elements → observe propagation → solve a constraint → unlock a tougher ecosystem.
- **Why fun might work:** Mechanical prediction creates a visible, satisfying 'aha' with a living payoff rather than abstract scoring.
- **Distinctive clip:** Single tile rotation changes an entire botanical structure from dormant to alive.
- **Content demand:** Physics/grid puzzle logic, puzzle editor, 20–30 handcrafted test boards, 4–5 palette families.
- **Business hypothesis:** Rewarded hint or paid finite puzzle packs; recurring play depends on novel levels/daily patterns.
- **Critical concern:** Light/pipe/laser puzzles already exist. Complexity and true differentiation must be verified.
- **Kill test:** New players cannot understand why their solution failed, or solve multiple levels without joy.
- **Benchmark family:** Mini Metro / The Gray Square / shapez (different rules; systems readability).

## C02 — Caseboard: The Missing Minutes
**Genre:** detective deduction + narrative logic  |  **Art:** illustrated noir with crisp information design  |  **Sessions:** 8–15 minutes.
- **Promise:** Solve small mysteries by finding contradictions among maps, timelines, messages and witness statements.
- **One action:** Connect two pieces of evidence to assert a logical contradiction.
- **Loop:** Receive incident → review evidence → test hypothesis → gain consequences → reveal a new case/lead.
- **Distinctive clip:** Two mundane statements snap together to reveal a lie, opening a new location on the timeline.
- **Content demand:** Fair mysteries, annotated evidence database, localisation, hint ladder, accessibility text design.
- **Business hypothesis:** Free opening case followed by paid case packs or optional rewarded hints.
- **Critical concern:** Expensive writing; content consumed once, replayability may be low; 'aha' must be fair.
- **Kill test:** Testers brute-force combinations, or describe resolutions as arbitrary rather than earned.
- **Benchmark family:** Tiny Room Stories / mystery and deduction games; audit five direct comparators later.

## C03 — Silver Screen Studio
**Genre:** film-production tycoon  |  **Art:** stylized isometric miniatures or clean illustrated management UI  |  **Sessions:** 10–20 minutes.
- **Promise:** Direct your own small film studio by balancing artistic decisions, budgets, crew and audiences.
- **One action:** Choose the trade-off for a shoot (spend, time, craft, or creative risk).
- **Loop:** Choose screenplay → allocate crew → shoot decisions → release → audience reaction → grow studio.
- **Distinctive clip:** Same premise produces wildly different poster, response and career outcomes depending on player decisions.
- **Content demand:** Economic balance, generated feedback, event variation, cast pool, visual progress feedback.
- **Business hypothesis:** Premium title or expansion content; reward ads would feel intrusive if not carefully designed.
- **Critical concern:** High UI complexity; differentiating from Game Dev Tycoon requires genuinely film-specific systems.
- **Kill test:** Playtest shows spreadsheet clicking without an enjoyable player decision each turn.
- **Benchmark family:** Game Dev Tycoon / Good Pizza, Great Pizza (structure only).

## C04 — Night Frequency
**Genre:** signal puzzle + narrative mystery  |  **Art:** cinematic minimalist radio console, layered audio and illustrated locations  |  **Sessions:** 5–12 minutes.
- **Promise:** Reconnect a quiet city by repairing unusual radio signals and uncovering why voices disappeared.
- **One action:** Slide and shape signal segments to eliminate interference while preserving a unique message.
- **Loop:** Tune signal → decode pattern → reveal message → choose destination → unlock next broadcast.
- **Distinctive clip:** Static resolves into a recognizable voice pattern and a city light turns on.
- **Content demand:** Original audio cues, sound-off visual equivalence, narrative episodes, puzzle generator.
- **Business hypothesis:** Free chapter with premium additional chapters; careful accessibility for hearing differences.
- **Critical concern:** Audio-first premise is poor for silent phone play; need sound-off mechanical clarity.
- **Kill test:** Puzzle is not enjoyable without sound, or interaction too opaque to describe in one sentence.
- **Benchmark family:** Narrative puzzle/mystery and systemic puzzle games; further direct comp research required.

## C05 — Parcel District
**Genre:** cozy routing / light management  |  **Art:** brightly colored illustrated map with toy-like postal shapes  |  **Sessions:** 3–8 minutes.
- **Promise:** Build the smartest routes for a lovable miniature neighborhood to deliver oddly shaped parcels on time.
- **One action:** Draw a delivery route or reroute vehicles on a simple map.
- **Loop:** Receive orders → plan route → watch consequences → solve bottleneck → expand district.
- **Distinctive clip:** A tangled route is fixed with one elegant redraw and the whole neighborhood starts moving.
- **Content demand:** Satisfying traffic/path planning, varied goals, ~30 test boards, upgrade economy.
- **Business hypothesis:** Rewarded retries or cosmetic districts, optional premium city packs.
- **Critical concern:** Visibly distinct from Mini Metro / Mini Motorways; complexity can overwhelm mobile touch UI.
- **Kill test:** Routes solve themselves or player cannot perceive the cause of failure.
- **Benchmark family:** Mini Metro (NOT a copy; forbid subway-line solution cloning).

## C06 — The Last Lantern
**Genre:** atmospheric puzzle-platform adventure  |  **Art:** layered cinematic silhouette / illustrated 2D  |  **Sessions:** 10–20 minutes.
- **Promise:** Restore lost memories to a twilight world using moving shadows as physical platforms.
- **One action:** Change lantern position/direction to change traversable geometry.
- **Loop:** Enter tableau → interpret shadow path → traverse puzzle → unlock story beat → new tableau.
- **Distinctive clip:** A character walks across the shadow of a tree as if it were a bridge.
- **Content demand:** Custom art and levels, collision physics, transitions, animation, original sound design.
- **Business hypothesis:** Paid mini-adventure or episodic packs; advertising is a poor fit for immersion.
- **Critical concern:** Hand-crafted art, puzzle fairness and mobile controls may overwhelm first-game scope.
- **Kill test:** Players fight movement/camera more than solve the shadow problem.
- **Benchmark family:** Atmospheric indie puzzle-adventures; more comparator diligence required.

## C07 — The Odd Shift
**Genre:** anomaly / observation mystery  |  **Art:** stylized single-room 2D with staged lighting  |  **Sessions:** 3–6 minutes.
- **Promise:** Inspect an evolving night-shift workplace and report subtle changes before the end of a shift.
- **One action:** Inspect the scene and correctly identify an impossible change.
- **Loop:** Scan room → notice anomaly → mark category → survive night → unlock new rules.
- **Distinctive clip:** Ordinary scene becomes uncanny with a single object change in a familiar space.
- **Content demand:** Dozens of high-quality variants, fair difficulty, accessible contrast, content repetition prevention.
- **Business hypothesis:** Ads between complete rounds / premium new rooms.
- **Critical concern:** Content costly, novelty can fade fast, and the space has established anomalies games.
- **Kill test:** Players repeatedly guess, cannot distinguish intended anomalies, or exhaust all content immediately.
- **Benchmark family:** Anomaly games; direct audits pending.

## C08 — Snapline Sprint
**Genre:** one-finger arcade + timed routing  |  **Art:** bold motion graphics / vector neon  |  **Sessions:** 30–120 seconds.
- **Promise:** Draw one perfect line through moving obstacles, then watch a tiny courier race it.
- **One action:** Swipe to draw a path within a short planning window.
- **Loop:** See course → draw route → run simulation → beat score / adapt → retry instantly.
- **Distinctive clip:** 2 seconds of drawing produces a dramatic near-miss and satisfying recovery.
- **Content demand:** Procedural course generation, collision fairness, UI feedback, daily challenge seed system.
- **Business hypothesis:** Voluntary rewarded continue, cosmetic trails and simple no-ads purchase.
- **Critical concern:** Extremely crowded arcade market; long-term return reasons weak without differentiated meta.
- **Kill test:** One-trick novelty: testers enjoy a first round but do not willingly attempt a third.
- **Benchmark family:** Mobile arcade/route-planning; competitors to be identified.

## Opportunity groups to investigate — NOT a ranking
- **Mechanic-led, visual repeatability:** C01 Lumen Grove, C05 Parcel District, C08 Snapline Sprint.
- **Narrative-led, differentiated positioning:** C02 Caseboard, C04 Night Frequency, C07 Odd Shift.
- **Systems-led, long-tail progression:** C03 Silver Screen Studio.
- **Art-led, stronger creative statement but greater asset burden:** C06 The Last Lantern.

## What is NOT established
- Whether any concept is novel in a trademark/copyright/market sense.
- Whether intended players would install or return.
- Exact ads/IAP, sales potential or release costs.
- Actual production effort, asset pipeline or remote APK build feasibility.
- Which one we'll make. **No winner.**
